# Proof review and correspondence with the paper

This is an AI code review. The reviewer read the paper and the Lean modules for polar integration, radial moments, Hilbert space reconstruction, cross-degree orthogonality, harmonic approximation, and passage to the limit. Other AI contributors wrote those modules. The reviewer also contributed the polynomial decomposition, maximum principle, and finite-mode calculations, so the discussion of those calculations is a proof-route description by their contributor. The authors of the paper have not been recorded as reviewers. The machine checks and their output are in `verification/`.

## Fixed annulus

| Step in the paper | Comparison with Lean |
| --- | --- |
| Null powers span the solid harmonics | The differential identity for null powers and the reverse inclusion are separate lemmas. The reverse inclusion uses the algebraic dual of the polynomial space. Mathlib’s multinomial coefficient formula identifies a functional on powers with evaluation of its symbol. The symbol vanishes on the null quadric. Lean proves the quadratic polynomial is squarefree in every dimension under consideration, including dimension two, and uses the Nullstellensatz to obtain divisibility. The adjoint relation between multiplication by the squared radius and the Laplacian concludes the proof. The paper proves the same divisibility by division in the last variable and uses a Hermitian Fischer pairing. |
| Necessary moments | A null vector gives one admissible interior polynomial and one decaying exterior Kelvin mode. The explicit bilinear gradient identity produces a real Laurent monomial times the angular power. The polar change of variables places the Jacobian in the radial measure. Conjugation is used at the angular coefficient, and division by the squared angular norm allows an orthogonal basis of any normalization. |
| Existence and convergence of the expansion | The formal argument first obtains a measurable angular L² class for almost every radius. Orthogonal projection, completeness, and integration of the squared angular error give convergence by degree in annular L². The polar map transfers both square integrability and the actual error norms. In particular, the conclusion does not demand a pointwise expansion of an arbitrary L² representative. |
| Decomposition of each gradient product | The identity for iterated Laplacians of a product gives the cutoff at the smaller of the two polynomial degrees. Successive removal of the highest radial power constructs the truncated Fischer decomposition. Euler’s formula and evaluation of the polynomial numerator give the exact coefficient from the paper. The coefficient vanishes at the interior-degree endpoint and, in dimension two, at the exterior-degree endpoint as well. These cancellations produce the two different summation ranges. |
| Sufficiency for general harmonic tests | The paper invokes convergent interior and exterior harmonic series with uniform convergence of the derivatives on the fixed annulus. The formalization uses finite sums from the same two mode spaces. Fischer decomposition and Stone–Weierstrass give approximation of the boundary values. The maximum principle extends that approximation into the ball or exterior. In dimension two a logarithmic barrier controls the bounded exterior difference. In higher dimensions decay controls the outer boundary. A local energy estimate applied on a slightly larger compact subset gives convergence in annular L² of each coordinate derivative. |

The last step uses two successive limits. First fix one exterior approximant and approximate the interior function. Then hold the interior function fixed and approximate the exterior function. At each step the fixed derivative is bounded on the compact annulus. Its product with the input belongs to L², so Cauchy–Schwarz bounds the error. Merely multiplying two sequences of L²-convergent derivatives would give insufficient control for a general L² input. The formalization does not assert uniform convergence of arbitrary derivative expansions.

## Varying radii

The equivalence between the volume and sphere clauses uses the polar identity and the vanishing of a continuous function whose integral on every subinterval vanishes. For necessity of the angular classification, the argument selects one available power from each constrained degree and applies the same one-dimensional principle to its continuous angular moment. In dimensions at least three this removes every positive degree. In dimension two it removes the degrees at least two. Density of harmonic restrictions, orthogonality of different degrees, and full support of surface measure identify each continuous angular slice with its low-degree part at every point. The planar conversion to exponentials also gives continuous profiles as functions of the radius.

The paper obtains this implication by invoking the fixed-annulus theorem on each subinterval. The Lean argument reuses the necessary-moment step from its proof. For the converse, each low-degree slice annihilates the separated tests in the constrained degrees. The finite-mode and approximation arguments then recover the original volume tests.

## Scope of review

The review compared the quantifier ranges, order of the limiting operations, conjugation conventions, endpoint cancellations, and use of continuity and almost-everywhere equality. It is distinct from a kernel check and from a human review. The exact status of the two final declarations is given by the build, comparator and axiom reports provided with this project.
